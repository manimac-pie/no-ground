End screen: design set

Same setup as the start screen sets: the game's 16:9 view, the start shell's type and colours, and the world as it is when a run ends. Bob hits the red ground, the claw reaches in from the left and drags him out, and the summary arrives as it grabs him. The wheel and the loose screws stay behind. Each version tallies the run, shows where it leaves you on the board, and ends on RESET.

Switch the outcome to see each version for an ordinary run, a new best that climbs the board, and a first run that makes the top 10 (naming yourself happens inline instead of in the pop-up). Click RESET in any frame to see the glitch.

OUTCOME
1

Shell printout

The start screen's shell, run in reverse. The same column on the sky now reads TERMINATED. The log says what happened, the shell runs tally and prints the run line by line, then records --week prints you with the players just above and below. The menu has the same spot and style: 01 RESET wakes up the way BREAK OUT does.

Good

  • The start and end screens become one system: the same column, the same voice, and the menu where you left it.
  • The world stays open on the right, so you watch the claw drag Bob off.
  • The board shows only your neighbours, which is the part that matters after a run.

Watch out

  • Plain text is quiet: a NEW BEST needs more than a gold line (a flash on the column, or the logo switching to NEW BEST).
  • The column is full once the claim line is in; the flavour lines would go first, as they do on the start screen.

Effort

  • Low–Medium. Most of it is shell.js with different lines. 02 EXIT TO SHELL is new (it goes back to the start screen without a run).
2

The hanging panel, rebuilt

Today's layout in the start screen's style. The panel still drops in on its rods with the rows tallying, the total capsule and the RESET prompt. The board still slides in beside it, and your row now climbs to its new rank. This is the "keep it, tidy it" baseline.

Good

  • Nothing new to learn for players, and most of the timing code (drop, tally, slide) already exists.
  • The climbing row turns a new best into something you see happen.

Watch out

  • Two big boxes cover most of the world; the claw scene plays behind them.
  • The rig is the only piece that doesn't match the shell's flat terminal look.

Effort

  • Low. Restyle summary.js and leaderboardPanel.js; the climb is a row offset that eases to 0.
3

Incident report

The system files the run. A document in the middle of a dimmed screen: subject, cause, distance and status in fields, the score breakdown in two columns, and a stamp that slams on once the tally is done: CONTAINED, NEW BEST or FIRST RECORD.

Good

  • Strong story: Bob is a case file and the score is the evidence. The stamp gives the end a clear beat.
  • One block to read, top to bottom, ending on the RESET button.

Watch out

  • The full board isn't here, only your standing line; it would need a "records" link.
  • Dims the world, so the claw drag is mostly hidden.

Effort

  • Medium. A new panel layout; the stamp is the existing NEW BEST stamp, bigger.
4

Rank climb

The board is the main event. The score card on the left tallies fast as chips. On the right, this week's board fills the screen and your row climbs past the players you beat, with a +2 PLACES badge and what it takes to pass the next one. On a short run your best holds, and a line says where this run would have placed.

Good

  • Makes the next goal obvious: the name above you and the points to pass them.
  • A short run still says something useful ("would place #11") instead of only "−17,074".

Watch out

  • The run's own breakdown is small; players who care about tricks get less.
  • Covers the world almost completely.

Effort

  • Medium. A larger board with the climb animation, and the "would place" lookup from the board data.
5

Black box

A flight recorder of the run along the top: one lane per kind of trick, with a mark wherever it happened. A playhead sweeps the run from 0 m to the red line where Bob hit the ground, and your best run's distance is a dashed amber line. The score sits in the middle of the sky with the RESET prompt under it.

Good

  • Shows where the run went right and where it ended, which a list of totals can't.
  • The sweep is the tally: the marks appear as the playhead passes them.

Watch out

  • Needs the game to record each trick's distance during the run (it only keeps counts today).
  • Busy runs (14 ads avoided) crowd a lane; marks would need to merge.

Effort

  • Medium–High. Event logging in score.js, plus the lanes panel.
6

Billboard verdict

The result goes up on a billboard on the next building, like the game's own ads: TERMINATED in red, NEW BEST in gold, FIRST RECORD in blue. The tally prints in a terminal window on the left, which ends on the RESET prompt.

Good

  • Uses the world's own language (billboards); the verdict reads from across the screen.
  • The colour of the billboard alone tells you how the run went.

Watch out

  • The billboard has to sit on whatever building is there at death; a fixed screen position is easier but less "in the world".
  • The board is reduced to a rank line.

Effort

  • Medium. A billboard drawn in screen space plus the terminal panel.
7

SIM NEWS

The system reports it on TV. The UI waits until the claw has dragged Bob out, so the death scene plays in full. Then a lower third comes up: BREAKING, a headline with how far he got, the score box with RESET, and a ticker crawling through the tally and the leaders.

Good

  • The death cinematic gets the whole screen first; the UI never blocks it.
  • Light: one strip along the bottom, the sky left empty.

Watch out

  • A ticker is slow to read: the breakdown takes a full crawl to see.
  • About a second more before RESET is ready than the other versions.

Effort

  • Low–Medium. One bottom panel and a scrolling text line.
8

Quick retry

For back-to-back runs: one small card at the bottom with the score, how it compares, your rank and a big RESET that's ready in about a second. The breakdown is one click away (DETAILS, or Tab).

Good

  • The fastest way back into a run; most players hammer Space anyway.
  • The world stays visible above it, wheel and screws included.

Watch out

  • No tally animation by default, so the trick bonuses are easy to miss.
  • It could be an option instead: the full screen the first few runs, the card after that.

Effort

  • Low. One card and a toggle for the breakdown.
