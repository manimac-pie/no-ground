Start screen: 4 directions

Static mockups drawn with HTML and SVG, laid out at the game's 16:10 frame. The world (roof, facade, Bob, the START firewall) stays the same in each. What changes is the UI around it.

What the current screen is missing

  • No game title. Nothing on screen says "No Ground" or hints at the story.
  • The leaderboard outweighs START. It's the biggest, brightest box, so the eye goes there first, not to the thing you're meant to break.
  • No "how to start" hint. New players don't know that jumping (Space / ↑ / tap) is what breaks the firewall.
  • TRAINING and GAME CONTROLS sit in opposite corners, though both are "help before you play". Their pill marker also looks like a toggle switch.
  • YOUR BEST repeats a leaderboard row (#2 31,279 appears twice), and the weekly board is folded away behind a small arrow.

What it fixes

  • Title and story hook (iteration count) up front.
  • Leaderboard is ~60% the size and dimmer, so START wins.
  • Your own row is highlighted in the board, not repeated.
  • Week / all-time is a visible tab, not a hidden arrow.

Trade-offs

  • Still a "menu on top of a game" feel.
  • The top-left corner gets busy on small phones; the tagline could hide there.

Effort

  • Low. Mostly moving existing canvas drawing (leaderboardPanel.js, training.js, controls.js) and adding a title and hint.

What it fixes

  • Tells the story before the first run: Bob is a subject, the system is counting his attempts.
  • One clear menu with START first, and keyboard hints.
  • All UI lives in one place, so the world stays clean.

Trade-offs

  • Covers a third of the world; Bob and the pane have to move right.
  • On portrait phones the panel would need to become a bottom sheet.
  • More text to read than the others.

Effort

  • Medium. New panel and menu hit areas in ui/layout.js, plus moving the start camera so Bob sits right of the panel.

What it fixes

  • Feels like part of the game, not a menu.
  • The leaderboard becomes "the system's ad", which fits the story and your existing billboards.
  • Your best sits on Bob, so it reads as his record.

Trade-offs

  • Hardest to keep readable: neon and billboards compete with the city.
  • The billboard has to scroll away with the world when the run starts (could be a nice moment).
  • Fewer leaderboard rows fit.

Effort

  • Medium–High. Reuse the reinforced billboard painter (world/billboards.js) for the board and add a neon sign sprite; both live in world space, not the HUD.
04

Cinematic

Letterbox bars frame the scene like a film's opening shot. Title and system status on top; menu and your progress on the bottom. The full leaderboard slides up from the bottom bar when opened (shown open here).

What it fixes

  • Very clear hierarchy: the middle is the game, the bars are UI.
  • Your progress is always visible in one line, the full board only when you want it.
  • The bars could slide away as Bob breaks the firewall, a strong transition into the run.

Trade-offs

  • The bars hide the top of the sky and some of the facade.
  • The leaderboard is one tap away instead of always on screen, so fewer players will see it.

Effort

  • Low–Medium. Two bars and a drawer in the HUD; the drawer can reuse panelCache.js. The bar exit animation is extra.
