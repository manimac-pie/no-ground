Shell: four more layouts

Built from the same pieces as start-screen-panel-positions.html: the log, the 01/02/03 menu, your best, the podium cards and this week's score bars. Every layout leaves the band where the claw comes in open, so the arm shows in full (about 107 units at 16:9), and the world is where it is in the game today: Bob at his usual spot, nothing moved.

A

Corner cards

Two floating cards in the top corners, the sky between them open. Left: the shell (log, menu, your best). Right: the records.

Good

  • Light: the world shows between and around the cards.
  • The log and the menu stay together, so the story leads straight into BREAK OUT.
  • The data link runs from BREAK OUT down onto the firewall.

Watch out

  • Two separate things to read, at both ends of the screen.
  • On very wide screens the cards drift far apart; they'd need a max distance.

Effort

  • Medium. Two cached panels instead of one; no camera change.
B

Ticker + rail

A slim bar across the top with the title and the menu side by side, and a one-line ticker under it that types the log out line by line. The records live in a rail down the right edge.

Good

  • The most world on screen: the bar is only about 60 units tall.
  • The ticker keeps the story going in one line: the last run stays pinned, the newest system line replaces the one before.
  • The rail has room for the podium as full-width cards.

Watch out

  • The log is never shown whole; only its newest line.
  • The rail eats into the right edge, where the firewall's far side is (it still fits at 16:9).

Effort

  • Medium. Two panels plus the ticker's line-by-line text.
C

In the world

No overlay panels. The shell is a terminal window floating in the sky, the records are one of the system's billboards on a tower, and the menu is a maintenance panel bolted to the roof's front edge, under Bob.

Good

  • Feels like part of the city, and the billboard fits the ads Bob smashes through.
  • The menu sits right next to Bob and the firewall: the link to the glass is the shortest of all.

Watch out

  • The billboard and the panel are in the world, so they'd scroll away with it on BREAK OUT (here they slide off instead).
  • The panel covers the roof's front face, where the building's top floor shows.

Effort

  • Medium–High. The billboard and the panel are drawn in world space, like the firewall.
D

Dock + drawer

Minimal: the title and log sit straight on the sky (no box), the menu is a small dock in the bottom-right corner, and the records are a drawer on the right edge. Click RECORDS to open and close it.

Good

  • The calmest screen when the drawer is shut: just the title, Bob and the firewall.
  • The dock is where thumbs and mice usually go for "play".

Watch out

  • Text straight on the sky needs a dark fade behind it to stay readable.
  • With the drawer shut, players won't see the leaderboard unless they open it.

Effort

  • Medium. Three small panels and a drawer slide.
E

Sky shell + LED ticker

D's title and log straight on the sky, with BREAK OUT, TRAINING and CONTROLS right under them (no box). The leaderboard leaves the UI: the full board crawls across the building's LED band, and a small chip top right shows just your standing and the one rival to beat. Hover the ticker to pause it.

Good

  • Everything you act on sits in one column: the story, then the three choices.
  • The board feels like the system's broadcast: the same news crawl everyone in the city sees.
  • The chip answers the question players actually have ("who do I beat next?") without a table.

Watch out

  • A crawl is slow to read: rank 10 can be 20+ seconds away. The chip covers what matters most.
  • The ticker sits on the building, so it covers the top floor's windows, and in the game it would scroll away with the world on BREAK OUT.
  • The menu column ends just above the line where the claw comes in.

Effort

  • Medium. The title and menu are one panel without a frame; the ticker is a world-space strip (like the firewall) with a scrolling clip.
