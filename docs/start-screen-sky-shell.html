Sky shell: leaderboard versions

The shell stays as it is in design E: the title, the log and the 01/02/03 menu straight on the sky, with no box. What changes is the leaderboard. The claw comes in fully visible in every version, and the world is where it is in the game today.

1

Records log

The board as the shell's twin: unboxed terminal text in the top-right corner, right-aligned, with dot leaders like a printout. The all-time top 3 and the gap to the next rank close it off.

Good

  • Matches the shell exactly: same type, same fade, mirrored. The screen reads as one system.
  • Shows the whole week (1–10) at once, no scrolling.

Watch out

  • Two text blocks in the top corners can feel busy; the sky between them has to stay empty.
  • Plain text needs the dark fade behind it over bright buildings.

Effort

  • Low. One more frameless panel, drawn like the log.
2

Score skyline

This week's top 5 as towers on the skyline to the right of the firewall: the taller the tower, the higher the score. #1 gets a gold roof and a blinking aerial; your tower is cyan. The all-time top 3 float above as a hall of fame.

Good

  • The city is the leaderboard: the gaps between players are visible at a glance.
  • Your tower next to #1's makes "beat the one above" physical.

Watch out

  • Only the top 5 fit (plus your tower if you're lower); ranks 6–10 would need a list or a hover.
  • Heights are relative (#5 to #1), so a close race looks bigger than it is.

Effort

  • Medium. Towers drawn in world space behind the roof; their heights come from the board.
3

Hologram

The records projected into the sky by an emitter on the roof, with a light cone and a faint flicker. Podium as stacked cards, then this week 1–10.

Good

  • Clearly part of the simulation: a projection the system puts up, not a menu.
  • Full board with room to breathe, and the shell keeps the left side to itself.

Watch out

  • The flicker should be subtle (and off with reduced motion) or it's tiring.
  • The emitter sits on the starter roof, so it would scroll away on BREAK OUT, taking the hologram with it.

Effort

  • Medium. One translucent panel plus the emitter and cone in world space.
4

Lower third

A TV-news strip along the bottom, over the building's lower floors: a SIM NEWS tag, this week's top 3 as podium cards, and your standing with the rival to beat. The full board would open from the strip.

Good

  • Fits the system-broadcast idea, and keeps the whole sky for the shell and the claw.
  • Shows only what matters at a glance: who's on top and how far you are from the next rank.

Watch out

  • Ranks 4–10 aren't on screen; they'd need the strip to open into the full board.
  • Covers the building's lower floors (the city's detail, not the action).

Effort

  • Low–Medium. One bottom panel; the full board as a pop-up from it.

The shell on the right, and more leaderboards

Same sky shell, now also tried top right (versions 5–7 and 9). With the shell on the right, the leaderboard takes the top left, where the sky is open above the claw.

5

Shell right · score line

Every score this week is a pin on one line along the bottom, from 0 to the all-time record. #1 is gold, you're cyan, and the all-time top 3 are hollow diamonds at the far end: you see how far everyone got, and how far the record is.

Good

  • Shows the gaps for real: close scores sit close together, and the record is visibly far ahead.
  • Keeps the whole sky free; the line sits on the building's lower floors.

Watch out

  • Close scores crowd each other; labels are staggered over three rows to stay readable.
  • Ranks aren't numbered: the order is the position on the line.

Effort

  • Medium. One bottom panel; labels placed by score with the staggering.
6

Shell right · monitor wall

The system's surveillance feed: this week's top 3 on big screens and ranks 4–10 on small ones, with a blinking REC. Your screen glows cyan. "The system is watching", literally.

Good

  • Fits the story: the leaderboard is the system watching its subjects.
  • All ten ranks visible, with the top 3 clearly bigger.

Watch out

  • Busy: ten screens is a lot of small boxes. Fewer (top 3 + you) would be calmer.
  • Screen text is small at phone sizes.

Effort

  • Medium. A grid of cached screens; the screen look is a few gradients.
7

Shell right · gap ladder

This week as a ladder you climb: one rung per rank, and between each pair of rungs the points it takes to move up. Your rung says YOU ARE HERE, and the gap above you is lit.

Good

  • Turns the board into goals: every rung shows what the next step costs.
  • Light: text on the sky, matching the shell's style.

Watch out

  • The gap numbers are one more thing to read; they could show only around your rung.

Effort

  • Low. A frameless list with the gaps worked out from the board.
8

Shell left · subject files

No table: three files the system keeps. TARGET is the one rank above you, YOU is your record, LEGEND is the all-time #1. "ALL FILES ▸" would open the full board.

Good

  • Tells a story with the numbers: who to beat next, where you are, and the record above it all.
  • Each file has room for flavour (status, attempts).

Watch out

  • The full top 10 is one click away, not on screen.
  • TARGET changes as you climb; at #1 it would become "DEFENDING".

Effort

  • Low–Medium. Three small panels; the full board as a pop-up.
9

Shell right · the shell prints the records

One panel only. After the boot log, the shell runs records --week and prints the top 5, then the all-time top 3, and the menu comes last. Everything on screen is the system talking.

Good

  • The simplest screen: one column of text, the rest is the world and the claw.
  • Reads top to bottom like a terminal session: what happened, who's ahead, what to do.

Watch out

  • A tall column: the menu ends up low, next to the firewall.
  • Only the top 5 + you; "5 more" would need the full list.

Effort

  • Low. The same shell panel with more log lines.
10

Shell left · terminal, records, menu

Version 9 on the left, in one column: the NO GROUND terminal, then the records (the all-time top 3, then this week's top 10 in two columns of five), then BREAK OUT, TRAINING and CONTROLS. The records type themselves in after "BOB: let me out.", as if the shell just ran the command. The records type in quickly right after "BOB: let me out.", and the menu sits at the bottom of the column, just above the roof. Bob stands further right so the column clears him.

Good

  • Reads top to bottom like one terminal session: what happened, who's ahead, what to do.
  • The full top 10 fits, and the sky over the firewall stays empty.
  • The typed-in printout gives the screen a last beat after the claw lets go.

Watch out

  • The column is tall, so the menu sits low: the claw passes behind it on its way in (it's gone by the time BREAK OUT wakes up).
  • Bob stands further right than in the run, so the camera eases back during the zoom-out (like the earlier left panel).
  • The printout should only type in the first time per session (it's quick, about 0.4 s, but still a wait).

Effort

  • Low–Medium. One frameless panel with more log lines on a timer, plus Bob's start position.
