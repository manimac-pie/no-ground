Start screen: terminal versions

Design 01's title block, with five ways to show the system's terminal and the scoreboard. They're all drawn on the same world as start-screen-mockups.html. Each one works: click ⏻ TERMINATE BOB, hold the red button, and the frame glitches back as a fresh iteration 0001 with no operator.

Story change: you are the operator

  • The player is the system's operator, the one running the simulation. Bob stays Bob: the subject, the one trying to get out.
  • The name you register is the operator's: operator MANIMAC @ sector-00. Scores go on the board under the operator.
  • TERMINATE BOB wipes the experiment. Bob goes back to iteration 0001 and the operator signs out. There's no name until a run makes the top 10 again (this week's top 10, or the all-time top 3, the same rule the board uses now). Only then does the name prompt open. Old records stay under the old name.
  • Bob gets the last word in the confirm: BOB: termination will not free me. Afterwards he's back with BOB: told you.

01

Window

The one from before: a dark terminal window on the sky, between the title and the roof. The log is on the left and the board on the right.

Good

  • Readable over anything: the window has its own dark backdrop.
  • It matches the end screen's console, so the start and end screens look like the same machine.

Less good

  • It's the heaviest version. Together with the title block, the left half reads as a menu.
  • Bob and the firewall have to move right to make room.
02

Clear

The same text and the same place, but with no window: the log and the board are printed straight onto the sky, like the game's start screen now. A dark shadow behind the text keeps it readable.

Good

  • The lightest version: the city shows through, and START stays the focus.
  • It's how the game's start screen already draws its log (shell.js), so it's the least new code.

Less good

  • The small text can get busy over lit windows. The shadow helps, but the board rows are the weakest spot.
  • The TERMINATE button needs its own dark plate to stand out.
03

Split clear

Clear, but in two pieces. The log stays under the title, and the records go to the top right, above the firewall, where 01 had them. There are no tabs: the all-time top 3 are at the top, then this week's top 10 with the purge countdown. Bob and the firewall can stay near the middle.

Good

  • Each piece sits where it belongs: what's happening under the title, the records off to the side.
  • It keeps the world balanced: Bob and the firewall are near the middle, the text is around the edges.

Less good

  • The eye has two places to look before it reaches START.
  • 13 rows down the right side is a lot of text on a small phone. The week could drop to 5 there.
04

Facade screen

Off the roof: the terminal is a screen built into the building's face, below the roof line, with a bezel and scanlines. The sky stays empty apart from the title, so Bob and the firewall go back to 01's positions.

Good

  • The most "in the world" version: it's the system's own display, bolted into its own building.
  • The sky and the roof are left to Bob and START.

Less good

  • Not much height: 5 rows per board, and YOUR BEST is dropped.
  • The confirm takes over the whole screen, so the board hides while you confirm.
  • It has to scroll away with the building when the zoom-out starts.
05

Dock

Off the roof too, but as UI rather than part of the world: a tmux-style strip along the bottom edge with three panes (log, this week, all-time). Its status bar holds the operator's name and TERMINATE.

Good

  • It mirrors the end screen: the console drops in from the top when Bob dies, and here it sits at the bottom.
  • It's the widest version, so both boards show at once with no tabs.

Less good

  • It covers the building's face, which is what shows it's the glass/steel kind.
  • On touch screens the bottom edge is where thumbs and the jump button go.
