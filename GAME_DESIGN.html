01Overview

You play Bob, a small white robot on one wheel who never stops rolling across a city at night. Touch the ground and the run ends. Most rooftops crack, sink or fall away, so every landing only buys you a moment.

GenreEndless runner / arcade platformer
PlatformWeb, desktop and mobile. Installable as a PWA.
OrientationLandscape first. 800×450 base, widens up to 1600 on wide screens.
SessionShort runs with an instant restart
TechVanilla JS and Canvas 2D, no build step
OnlineAnonymous Top 10 leaderboard on a Cloudflare Worker

Design pillars

Keep movingRoofs build up stress while you stand on them and then collapse. Standing still is never safe.
Readable dangerEvery threat has a tell: cracks, sinking roofs, and billboards marked "reinforced" or not.
Expressive airFive verbs let skilled players do more than survive. They can add style and extra score.
Fair randomnessThe generator checks every gap can be cleared at the current speed. Grace windows make deaths feel earned.

03Player & controls

Bob stays at a fixed screen x and the world scrolls past him. The player controls only his vertical movement and the world's speed (with dash).

Bob

Bob is drawn in code (src/render/playerBody.js) inside a 34×34 hitbox: a white capsule body, a dark visor with one cyan eye that darts around, and a spoked wheel whose speed follows the world speed.

04Movement

Each verb changes Bob's flight in a different way. These arcs come from simulating the game's own physics constants at the chosen base speed.

260 px/s
"Clears" compares the distance covered by the time Bob drops back to take-off height with the largest gap the generator can make: 150 px early on, 220 px at full difficulty. Jump is held the whole time and slowfall starts at take-off.

05World & hazards

Rooftops are 160–360 px wide, with gaps of 56 px up to 150–220 px. Roofs sit on five height levels above a glowing red ground line. The generator never makes more than two breakable roofs in a row, or more than two solid ones.

Stress collapseA breakable roof collapses after you stand on it for 1.05 s early on, or 0.55 s late. Diving while on the roof builds stress 3.25× faster. A heavy landing adds 22–34% at once.
Rising roofStarts low and rises to its resting height. It only starts moving once Bob is in the air and the roof is 40–520 px ahead.
Crumbling roofSinks by up to about 60 px while you're in the air. Some are set to break away completely. They only break once you're more than 90 px from the edge, so they never drop out from under a landing.
Reinforced buildingA safe place to rest. 40% of buildings early on, falling to 5% at full difficulty.
Billboard, non-reinforced (red)Dash or dive through it to smash it for +130. Hitting its side without dashing is fatal.
Low billboard (hazard stripes)Hangs at head height, 27 px above the roof, and is 120 px tall (taller than regular billboards), so a single jump can't clear it. Hold S to duck under it (+0.25 s) or double jump over it. Standing up underneath is fatal. 45% of billboards are low when the roof is wide enough (226 px or more).
Billboard, reinforced (blue)Go over it (+0.15 s) or duck under it (+0.25 s, only while ducking), or land on top: it works as a platform and gives 1.2× distance. Hitting its side is fatal.

About every 5th building has a 50% chance of a billboard above it.

Guided air challenges

Every 650–1,170 px, the generator may shape the next gap (10–28% chance) so that it needs a particular move.

Slowfall gapThe gap is 78–98% of the maximum, with a slight drop. Slowfalling keeps Bob in the air long enough to reach the far roof.
Dive gapA short gap (66–106 px) with a steep drop of 90–170 px. Diving gets Bob down onto the low roof quickly.

06Difficulty

Two ramps run together. World speed rises with time: +6 px/s every second, reaching the 480 cap at about 37 s. Every other lever follows a 0–1 difficulty value based on distance, which peaks at 10,000 px, about 29 s into a run without dashes.

Dashing covers distance faster, so players who dash a lot reach full difficulty sooner. There are no levels or unlocks: the player's own skill and the leaderboard are the only progression.

07Scoring

Score is distance in pixels plus bonuses. Distance makes up most of it. Tricks and bonuses decide close runs.

Bonuses are measured in seconds of running. A bonus of 0.25 s is worth 0.25 × the current speed (dash boost not counted): 65 points at the start, 120 at top speed. That way bonuses keep the same weight against distance for the whole run. Points only go to moves with some risk or skill. A plain jump scores nothing, and a dash only scores in the air.

Air pot. From takeoff to landing, distance and bonuses go into a pot for that jump instead of the score. A safe landing (roof or billboard top) pays out air distance × air multiplier + bonuses. The air multiplier is 1 + 0.5 per backflip + 0.5 per chain link, capped at ×4, and only applies to distance. A jump with no backflip is ×1. Dying before landing loses the pot. Each backflip pops up its new multiplier, and the HUD shows the pot and multiplier next to the score while airborne.

Chain. A clean tricked landing (at least one backflip, none still spinning) adds a chain link. A plain landing or a mid-flip landing ("SLOPPY") resets it. A sloppy landing still pays its flips, but not the chain. The HUD shows CHAIN ×n between jumps.

Slowfall has no multiplier. Fuel refills on every landing, so a slowfall multiplier was a free ×2 on every jump. Slowfall earns through airtime instead: more distance in the pot, and room for another flip.

Bonus per event, in seconds of running. Air bonuses go into the pot. For scale, one second at top speed is worth 480 distance points.

Run summary and leaderboard

Mock-up with example values (the live panel now has six rows: distance, trick multiplier, backflips, billboards broken, close calls, other bonuses). The rows tally one at a time, 0.3 s each (0.1 s for a row worth nothing): each row counts up, then its points fly into the total. A press finishes the tally at once.
Each player is an anonymous device ID stored in localStorage. There are no accounts.

09Architecture

Game logic updates one state object at a fixed 60 Hz. The renderer only reads that object and never changes it.
